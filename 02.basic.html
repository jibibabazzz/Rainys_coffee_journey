COFFEE KNOWLEDGE

From Bean to Cup

Coffee has many stages before it becomes a drink. This page explains coffee beans, growing regions, processing, roasting, sensory tasting, and brewing in simple language.

01

What Coffee Is

Coffee is made from roasted coffee beans, which are seeds found inside coffee cherries. The flavour of coffee can change depending on bean variety, origin, climate, altitude, processing method, roast level, and brewing technique. Specialty coffee often focuses on traceability, quality, and flavour clarity from farm to cup.

02

Types of Coffee Beans

Arabica and Robusta are the two most common coffee species. Arabica usually has smoother acidity, sweeter flavour, and more complex aroma notes such as fruit, floral, or chocolate. Robusta contains higher caffeine and often has a stronger, more bitter taste with heavier body and earthy notes. Bean characteristics can also change based on growing regions such as Ethiopia, Colombia, Brazil, Kenya, Thailand, and Indonesia.

03

Harvesting and Processing

Coffee cherries are harvested when fully ripe to improve sweetness and flavour quality. After harvesting, processing methods are used to separate the coffee seed from the fruit. Common methods include washed, natural, and honey processing. Washed coffee often tastes cleaner and brighter, while natural coffee can produce fruitier and sweeter flavour profiles. Honey processing usually creates balanced sweetness and smooth body.

04

Roasting Basics

Roasting transforms green coffee beans into aromatic brown coffee beans through heat. Different roast levels can change flavour, aroma, and body. Light roast coffee usually highlights acidity and origin character, while medium roast creates balanced sweetness and body. Dark roast coffee often produces deeper chocolate, smoky, caramel, and bitter flavour notes.

05

Sensory Basics

Coffee sensory evaluation focuses on understanding flavour, aroma, acidity, sweetness, bitterness, aftertaste, body, and balance. These sensory terms help coffee professionals and consumers describe coffee clearly. Fruity coffees may show berry or citrus notes, while chocolate or nutty notes are common in medium and darker roasts. Sensory tasting and cupping are important practices in specialty coffee culture.
